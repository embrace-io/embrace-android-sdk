X12 — Prior Startup Findings Re-derived Under the Corrected Analysis Rules

A pure re-analysis of traces already on disk: no device was touched, no benchmark was run, no new data was produced. Four analysis defects were identified after the original startup campaigns were written up — a case-insensitive GC match, process-name-scoped counting, unclipped interval sums, and no data-loss check on any historical trace. This document re-derives the affected numbers, states which prior claims change, which are confirmed unchanged, and which could not be checked. Tracked as item X12 in the tracker.

Headline: the flagship claim survives. Own-process GC in Go-tier outlier windows re-derives to 79% (45/57 policy-defined outliers, n = 200 traces) against the previously published 82% (9/11 hand-picked windows) — and applying the corrected query to the same 11 windows reproduces 9/11 = 82% and every GC wall figure to the millisecond. The GC defect was real but it did not bite where the claim was made. It bit somewhere else entirely: on every ART-12-and-newer device, 100% of the own-GC flags in the published factor tables were fabricated by obfuscated class-load slices named Lgc; / Lgc0;. And the loss sweep found zero buffer-loss traces in 596 sampled — no historical conclusion rests on an evicted window.

Method and honest scopewhat was actually parsed, and what that does and does not license

  • Tool: pinned trace_processor v46.0 via .claude/skills/_shared/tooling.py. 620 trace_processor invocations against a ~600 budget (12 inventory + 12 smoke + 300 GC + 264 health + 32 .pftrace catch-up).
  • One combined query per trace (x12_main.sql) answers all three empirical priorities from a single parse: perfetto loss counters, window/thread anchoring, interval clipping, launch count, and the full GC taxonomy. That is why each campaign's GC population doubles as its loss sweep.
  • Corrected rules applied throughout: window and thread come from the same emb-sdk-start slice row and upid is taken from that thread (never from a process-name predicate); GC matched with case-sensitive GLOB '*GC*' and then classified by ART slice name; every slice and thread_state interval clipped MIN(end, win_end) − MAX(start, win_start); outliers defined by the run-shape policy (window − pass median > max(4 ms, 10% of pass median)) rather than by eye.
  • Every number below is a sample figure with its n stated inline. 596 of the 5,504 surviving traces were parsed (10.8%). Nothing here should be read as a population figure for a campaign, and no campaign was exhausted except verify-4x50/a01 (200/200 — deliberately, because that is the population the disputed claim was made on).
  • Excluded: bp-ab-INVALID-built-against-9.1.0 (320 traces, already quarantined).

1b. Window-clipped, on the Go tier, the two matches are identical

Every published D-class number was window-clipped and upid-scoped. At that scope, on the A01 Core (Android 10 / ART 10), LIKE and GLOB return exactly the same slices — 0 false positives, 0 fabricated milliseconds, across 316 A01 traces (200 verify-4x50 + 100 a01-campaign + 16 a01-mini). The reason is in the reference already: older ART generations emit no per-class Lfoo; slices, so the dominant false-positive family simply does not exist there, and the rest (art::gc::Heap::TrimSpaces, buildDrawingCache, scheduleAppGcs()) never overlapped an init window in this sample.

new findingA second correction to the GC count itself. Any count-shaped GC metric — including a trace-side grader for the shipped init-gc-count attribute — must filter to collection names (*copying GC / *mark compact GC / *mark sweep GC), not to *GC*.

verdictZero BUFFER loss in 596 traces across all 16 surviving campaigns, including both thermal/contention arms where saturation was most plausible. No prior conclusion drew on an evicted window. Every trace sampled also produced its window (0 window-missing), and every trace parsed cleanly (0 non-zero exit codes).

PARSE-level loss: 87/596 = 14.6%, concentrated by device rather than by arm — 100% of pixel-campaign and p15-pass2 (both Pixel 3), 50% of cooldown-campaign, 27–55% of the two Pixel 7 Pro campaigns, and 0/316 across the three A01 campaigns as well as 0 in a14-campaign2, ancient-history and version-smoke (bp-ab, which mixes devices, shows 7/24). Two counters dominate and they mean different things: mm_unknown_type is an unrecognised mm_event ftrace subtype and affects only memory-event counters (no slices, no sched, no thread_state), so it cannot touch any published figure. systrace_parse_failure and sorter_push_event_out_of_order can drop individual atrace slices — relevant to counting claims on the Pixel 7 Pro (where 11/20 traces carry them), not to window durations or thread-state shares. Practical consequence: the Pixel 7 Pro's slice-count-based observations (e.g. "no GC at all", ART slice inventories) should be stated as "none observed, with atrace parse failures present in 27–55% of that device's traces", while its window medians and thread-state splits are unaffected.

One methodological note worth carrying back into trace_health.py: run verbatim it would have called 524/596 of these traces "lossy" — 437 of them on power_rail_empty_packet and packages_list_has_read_errors alone, neither of which touches a slice. The bucketing above (BUFFER / PARSE / META) should be folded into that module, otherwise the gate is uninformative and will be ignored.

What could not be checked, and why

  • Foreign-process (system_server) GC overlap — the B1 heap-compaction figures. The combined query scopes GC to the app's own upid by design, so the ">5 ms of system_server heap-compaction GC → 13/13 slow" figure was not re-derived. The risk is concrete: the observed LIKE-only false positives in other processes include a system_server monitor-contention slice naming scheduleAppGcs(), which is not a collection. Re-deriving it needs a foreign-upid variant of the query (~1 h, no new data required).
  • Section-level durations and CPU/cluster figures were not re-derived. The anchoring and clipping audit says the published section tooling (variance_metrics.sql) is arithmetically sound, so there was no defect to correct — but that is a verification of the query, not a re-measurement of the numbers.
  • Any claim whose traces are gone. The induced-load causal arms, the E15 no-tracing TTID cycles, the X2/X6 dexopt discriminators and the thermal staircases live in logs, not in surviving traces; they can only be re-labelled (priority 4), never re-derived. Their strongest results are manipulation-based and are the ones least in need of it.
  • Population figures for any campaign except verify-4x50/a01. Everything else is a stratified sample of 16–100 traces out of 40–1,600. The loss verdict in particular is "0 BUFFER-loss in 596 sampled", which bounds but does not exclude a low prevalence: at n = 30 per arm, a defect affecting <10% of traces could still have been missed in the smaller campaigns.
  • version-history / version-smoke windows. Those SDK versions emit no emb-sdk-start, and this query's composed fallback is not the doc's composed window, so their durations were deliberately not recomputed. Re-deriving the version line under corrected rules needs the doc's own fallback definition (modules-init → post-services-setup).

5b. The corrected query, and how B1's literal "13 windows" were re-identified

The foreign-upid query lives at .claude/skills/startup-multi-device-analysis/scripts/foreign_gc_overlap.sql, reusing the emb-sdk-start window anchoring and MIN/MAX clipping this document already validated, scoped with th.upid != me.upid and grouped by process name, with three guards:

  • Guard 1 (case-sensitivity): GLOB not LIKE.
  • Guard 2 (collection, not substring): match names ending in GC, exclude Lock contention*.
  • Guard 3, new for foreign processes: exclude and separately count scheduleAppGcs* — a system_server call that schedules other apps' GC, not a collection it performed itself. All three claims — contention, scheduling, collecting — are reported as separate counted buckets (foreign_gc_collection_*, foreign_gc_lock_contention_*, foreign_scheduleAppGcs_*), plus the pre-guard raw counts (foreign_gc_like_raw_*, foreign_gc_glob_raw_*) for a before/after comparison — nothing is silently dropped.

The original T1a script also survives in the scratchpad (gc_overlap.sql) and reveals its exact predicate: name LIKE '%GC%' AND depth = 0, scoped to the process named system_server. That let the literal 13 windows be reproduced with zero new trace_processor invocations, straight from the already-computed passN-gc.json files: window medians per pass, delta per iteration, and every iteration with the old gc_ss_ms > 5 flag. Result: n = 13, mean delta +10.95 ms — matching the published "13/13, mean delta +10.9 ms" to the first decimal — and all 13 have delta > 0, confirming "every one slow" as originally stated (two of the 13, at +1.1 ms and +0.4 ms, are barely positive rather than outliers by the document's own >4 ms/10% policy — a minor looseness in the original "slow" framing, not a defect in the GC figure itself).
