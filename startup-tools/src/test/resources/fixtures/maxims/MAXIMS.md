# SDK startup maxims (bench)

What we currently believe about Android SDK init from the benchmark fleet, and the evidence for it. Generated by `maxims render` at 2026-09-04T21:30:00Z from the maxim definitions in `startup-tools` and a ledger of 2 runs (last updated 2026-09-04T21:00:00Z). Do not edit by hand: change a maxim in code or score another campaign, then regenerate. Conclusions that are not per-campaign checks are in [FINDINGS.md](FINDINGS.md). The production counterpart with the same structure is `tool/sdk-startup/MAXIMS.md` in the go repository; a maxim id that appears in both is the same claim tested on the fleet and on the bench.

**Scopes.** *Universal*: a claim about the SDK that should hold at the stated size on every device; one device contradicting it counts against it. *Directional*: the direction holds everywhere but the size is the device's, so a lift that points the right way but misses the floor is *undetected*, not a contradiction. *Device-specific*: the sign itself is the device's, and the split by device is the finding.

**Status.** *accepted*: every device that tested it holds it. *under review*: a device fails or is mixed but most hold it. *refuted*: as many devices fail it as hold it. *untested*: no verdict yet. A device *holds* a maxim when its confirmations outnumber contradictions two to one across every run, *fails* when the reverse. A cell is one campaign: one device under one recipe and arm.

## off-cpu

**A slow init is one whose main thread was off-CPU: the lowest quartile of on-CPU share is at least 1.5x as likely to be slow.**

- Scope: universal. Status: **refuted**.
- Why: Init is main-thread work; when the thread is not running, something else is holding it. Same claim as the production maxim, read from the trace's thread states instead of init-cpu-pct.
- Evidence: 1 confirmed, 1 contradicted, 0 thin cells over 2 runs; holds on 1 of 2 devices tested (1 fails).
- Latest contradictions (cell, observed):
  - flagship-a / 9.2.0 / profile: 1.33x on 15 iterations (slow 2/15 vs 6/60)

## scheduler-wait

**Any scheduler wait makes a slow init likelier, by 2x where the effect is measurable: the highest quartile of runnable-wait share.**

- Scope: directional. Status: **refuted**.
- Why: A runnable thread that is not scheduled loses wall time the SDK cannot recover; how often that happens is the device's. The bench proved the concurrent-CPU mechanism causal by inducing churn.
- Evidence: 0 confirmed, 1 contradicted, 0 thin, 1 undetected cells over 2 runs; holds on 0 of 2 devices tested (1 fails, 1 undetected).
- Latest contradictions (cell, observed):
  - flagship-a / 9.2.0 / profile: 0.00x on 15 iterations (slow 0/15 vs 6/60)

## concurrent-cpu

**Concurrent CPU from other processes makes a slow init likelier, by 1.5x or more where measurable: the highest quartile of other-process on-CPU share of the window, the idle task excluded.**

- Scope: directional. Status: **refuted**.
- Why: The bench's extreme-outlier class: system_server GC compactions, dexopt and app-ecosystem churn inflate the window through the memory bus while the main thread's own wait stays small. Proven causal by inducing churn; how much of it a device sees is the device's.
- Evidence: 1 confirmed, 1 contradicted, 0 thin cells over 2 runs; holds on 1 of 2 devices tested (1 fails).
- Latest contradictions (cell, observed):
  - flagship-a / 9.2.0 / profile: 0.00x on 15 iterations (slow 0/15 vs 6/60)

## starved-enriched

**Starved inits (runnable-wait share of 20% or more) are at least 3x enriched among slow inits.**

- Scope: universal. Status: **accepted**.
- Why: Rare on a quiet bench, and decisive when it happens; the production tool uses the same 20% cutoff.
- Evidence: 1 confirmed, 0 contradicted, 1 thin cells over 2 runs; holds on 1 of 1 devices tested.

## gc-rare

**The SDK's own GC almost never runs during init: a collection of 1 ms or more inside the window on 2% or fewer of iterations on any device above 2 GB of RAM.**

- Scope: universal. Status: **accepted**.
- Why: Own-process collection during init was only ever seen on the 1 GB tier; above it a zero is the device, not the detector. Production sees 0.02 to 0.3%. The 1 ms floor keeps sub-millisecond class-load slices misread as GC in older datasets from counting.
- Evidence: 2 confirmed, 0 contradicted, 0 thin cells over 2 runs; holds on 2 of 2 devices tested.

## first-launch

**The first launch after an install is slower than the rest of its pass, by 1.15x or more where measurable.**

- Scope: directional. Status: **refuted**.
- Why: Install aftermath: dexopt and system bursts. The bench also found the fresh-install config fast path can cancel it on fast tiers, which is why this is directional and why a contradiction here is expected on flagships.
- Evidence: 1 confirmed, 1 contradicted, 0 thin cells over 2 runs; holds on 1 of 2 devices tested (1 fails).
- Latest contradictions (cell, observed):
  - flagship-a / 9.2.0 / profile: iter000/rest 0.79x over 3 passes

## config-fast-path

**Every pass's first launch takes the fresh-install config fast path: its persisted-config-load is below the median of the rest of the pass.**

- Scope: universal. Status: **refuted**.
- Why: iter000 has no cached config to decode. A violation means app data survived a failed uninstall and the pass's first-launch sample is poisoned (hypothesis H3); the check is tier-relative because the two bands scale with the device. An arm that clears app data before every launch is n/a: every launch takes the fast path there, so the comparison has no second population.
- Evidence: 1 confirmed, 1 contradicted, 0 thin cells over 2 runs; holds on 1 of 2 devices tested (1 fails).
- Latest contradictions (cell, observed):
  - flagship-a / 9.2.0 / profile: 3 of 3 passes; pass 1: iter000 6.0 ms vs rest p50 3.5 ms

## compile-state-toggle

**Pass medians alternate between two levels at least 10% apart, with pure-CPU sections within 1.25x and block-and-resume sections 1.5x or more between the two states.**

- Scope: device-specific. Status: **accepted (device-specific)**.
- Why: Install-time compile state alternating per reinstall on some OEM builds (hypothesis H2). Which devices show it is the finding; compare only matching-state passes where they do.
- Evidence: 1 confirmed, 1 contradicted, 0 thin cells over 2 runs; holds on 1 of 2 devices tested (1 fails).
- Latest contradictions (cell, observed):
  - flagship-a / 9.2.0 / profile: pass medians 12.2 -> 12.7 -> 12.2; pure-CPU 1.00x, block-resume 1.00x

## restore-vs-create

**A launch that creates a user session spends at least 2x as long in start-first-session as one that restores the persisted session.**

- Scope: universal. Status: **accepted**.
- Why: The create path serializes and persists session metadata on the main thread; the restore path is a read. The bench measured only the restore path until the cohort tap existed. An arm that pins the cohort cannot test it - the comparison is then across the matching cells of the two arms, not inside one campaign, and the check reports n/a rather than pretending the sample is merely thin.
- Evidence: 1 confirmed, 0 contradicted, 0 thin cells over 2 runs; holds on 1 of 1 devices tested.

## cpu-closure

**The init span's CPU attributes close: init-cpu-pct plus init-run-delay-pct is between 0 and 100 on at least 98% of launches.**

- Scope: universal. Status: **accepted**.
- Why: The one attribute check that needs no ground truth; a violation means the attributes and the trace disagree and the run is not trustworthy.
- Evidence: 1 confirmed, 0 contradicted, 0 thin cells over 2 runs; holds on 1 of 1 devices tested.
